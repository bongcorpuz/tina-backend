**Case Identified** CTA Case No. 9711 – Dizon Farms Produce, Inc. v. CIR **Facts / Context** In this case, the taxpayer, Dizon Farms Produce, Inc., contested a deficiency tax assessment issued by the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR). The taxpayer argued that the assessment violated their due process rights as it was issued without the required pre-assessment notice (PAN). **Issue** The primary issue was whether the BIR's failure to issue a PAN prior to the assessment rendered the tax assessment null and void, thereby violating the taxpayer's due process rights. **Ruling / Holding** The Court of Tax Appeals (CTA) ruled in favor of the taxpayer, stating that the issuance of a PAN is a mandatory requirement for due process in tax assessments. The absence of a PAN rendered the assessment invalid. **Doctrine** The doctrine established in this case emphasizes that tax assessments must comply with due process requirements, specifically the issuance of a PAN, to be valid. The CTA referenced the principle that tax assessments issued in violation of a taxpayer's due process rights are null and void. **Application** In applying this doctrine, the CTA highlighted that the BIR must adhere to procedural requirements when assessing taxes. The failure to provide a PAN deprives the taxpayer of the opportunity to contest the findings before a formal assessment is made, which is a fundamental aspect of due process. **Status / Limits** This ruling underscores the importance of procedural compliance by the BIR in tax assessments. It limits the BIR's ability to issue assessments without following due process, thereby protecting taxpayers' rights. However, it does not address the substantive issues of tax liability, focusing instead on procedural safeguards. In conclusion, while the specific BIR revenue memorandum circular requested by the taxpayer could not be located, the principles established in CTA Case No. 9711 provide a clear framework regarding the necessity of due process in tax assessments, which is relevant to any taxpayer's rights in similar situations.
