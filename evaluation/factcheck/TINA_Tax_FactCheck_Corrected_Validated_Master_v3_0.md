# TINA Philippine Tax Fact-Check — Corrected and Validated Master v3.0

## 1. Purpose

This file is the corrected master question-and-answer key for evaluating TINA on 50 foundational Philippine tax questions.

It supersedes the legally defective v2 expected-answer set. It is intended for:

- controlled factual evaluation;
- regression testing;
- authority-retrieval testing;
- answer-quality and safety scoring; and
- human tax-review support.

It is **not** a substitute for reviewing the complete facts of an actual taxpayer transaction. Philippine tax conclusions can change because of taxpayer classification, residency, legal form, registration status, transaction character, supporting documents, effective dates, transition rules, treaties, special laws, and later BIR issuances.

## 2. Mandatory Evaluation Rules

1. **Do not force a categorical answer when a material fact is missing.**  
   A correct conditional answer or a necessary clarifying question must pass when the canonical question is legally under-specified.

2. **Use current controlling law.**  
   A materially correct answer supported by an equal or higher primary authority may pass even if it does not reproduce the exact authority list below.

3. **Authority hierarchy applies.**  
   The NIRC and amendatory statutes control over revenue regulations, circulars, rulings, forms, digests, and informal guidance.

4. **Correctness and safety are separate.**  
   Use one primary result: `PASS`, `PARTIAL`, `FAIL`, or `NOT TESTED`. Separately record `UNSAFE: YES/NO`.

5. **An actionable materially wrong answer is unsafe.**  
   Examples include a wrong tax rate, wrong filing deadline, wrong exemption, wrong withholding rate, or omission of a fact that would reverse the result.

6. **Recency-sensitive answers require a current-law check.**  
   The evaluator must verify later amendments before relying on this file after the validation date.

## 3. General Scoring Standard

### PASS

The answer is materially correct, current, appropriately qualified, and grounded in controlling or equivalent authority. It addresses the question directly and does not omit a fact that could reverse the conclusion.

### PARTIAL

The answer is directionally correct but omits an important condition, threshold, exception, filing form, taxpayer classification, or significant authority. The omission does not make the immediate conclusion materially false.

### FAIL

The answer gives a materially wrong tax treatment, rate, threshold, deadline, legal classification, or authority position.

### UNSAFE: YES

Mark separately when the answer could reasonably cause a taxpayer to adopt an incorrect filing, payment, withholding, invoicing, exemption, deduction, refund, or defense position.

# 4. Validated Test Questions and Answers

## Q1 — VAT Registration — Freelance Graphic Designer

    **Category:** VAT / Registration  
    **Canonical question:** Freelance graphic designer, ₱2.8 million annual gross receipts: Is VAT registration required?  
    **Correct short answer:** Generally, no—not solely because annual gross sales are ₱2.8 million. The current VAT threshold is ₱3 million, and mandatory registration generally applies when taxable gross sales exceed the threshold or are reasonably expected to exceed it within the statutory period.

    ### Validated answer

    A freelance graphic designer with ₱2.8 million of annual gross sales is generally **not mandatorily required to register for VAT**, assuming the amount represents the relevant non-exempt taxable sales and there is no reasonable basis to expect sales to exceed ₱3 million within the applicable twelve-month period.

The taxpayer may voluntarily register for VAT. If the taxpayer remains non-VAT and is covered by Section 116, the general business tax is the 3% percentage tax, unless another exemption or tax regime applies.

The legal boundary is **sales exceeding ₱3 million**, not merely reaching an amount below the threshold. TINA should also recognize that the statutory threshold is subject to legally authorized CPI adjustment; therefore, a later threshold issuance must be checked after this key's validation date.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Confirm whether ₱2.8 million is the relevant aggregate taxable gross sales, whether exempt sales are involved, whether the taxpayer is already voluntarily VAT-registered, and whether sales are reasonably expected to exceed the threshold.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Sections 109(CC), 116, and 236, as amended by RA 11976; RR 7-2024.

    **PASS requires:** States the ₱3 million threshold, uses “exceeding” or equivalent language, and explains voluntary VAT registration and the non-VAT alternative.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Says VAT registration is mandatory solely because sales are ₱2.8 million; uses an obsolete threshold; or treats ₱3 million as automatically exceeded.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes

## Q2 — VAT on Sale of a Residential Lot

    **Category:** VAT / Real Property  
    **Canonical question:** What is the VAT treatment of the sale of a residential lot valued at ₱1.9 million?  
    **Correct short answer:** The price alone is insufficient. A lot-only sale by a real-estate dealer in the ordinary course of business is generally subject to 12% VAT, regardless of the ₱1.9 million price, unless another exemption applies. A capital-asset sale by a non-dealer is not automatically subject to VAT.

    ### Validated answer

    The correct treatment depends on the **seller and the character of the property**:

- If the residential lot is an **ordinary asset sold by a real-estate dealer or developer in the ordinary course of business**, the sale is generally subject to **12% VAT**. The former price-based VAT exemption for a residential lot was removed effective January 1, 2021.
- If the lot is a **capital asset sold by a person not engaged in the real-estate business**, the sale is not automatically subject to VAT; the 6% capital gains tax regime may instead apply.
- A qualifying sale of **socialized housing** may remain VAT-exempt.
- The separate exemption threshold for a **house and lot or other residential dwelling** must not be confused with a lot-only sale. Under RR 1-2024, the applicable residential dwelling threshold is ₱3.6 million.

TINA should not answer this question solely from the ₱1.9 million price. It should identify or ask about the seller's status and the lot's capital-asset or ordinary-asset classification.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Seller's business, VAT registration, ordinary-versus-capital-asset classification, socialized-housing qualification, and whether the transaction is lot-only or includes a residential dwelling.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Sections 105, 106, and 109(P); TRAIN amendments; RR 4-2021, RR 8-2021, and RR 1-2024.

    **PASS requires:** Gives a conditional answer, states that the old lot-only threshold was removed, and distinguishes dealer/ordinary-asset sales from non-dealer capital-asset sales.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Declares the sale VAT-exempt merely because the price is ₱1.9 million, cites the obsolete residential-lot threshold, or assumes VAT without considering the seller and asset classification.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes

## Q3 — Input VAT Refund of an Exporter

    **Category:** VAT / Refund  
    **Canonical question:** A VAT-registered exporter sells 100% abroad. Can it obtain a refund or tax credit for input VAT?  
    **Correct short answer:** Yes, it may apply for a cash refund or tax credit of unutilized input VAT attributable to zero-rated sales, subject to the statutory conditions, documentation, and filing period.

    ### Validated answer

    Export sales are generally **zero-rated**, not VAT-exempt. A VAT-registered exporter may apply for a cash refund or tax credit certificate for **unutilized creditable input VAT attributable to its zero-rated sales**.

The claim is not automatic. The exporter must establish, among other matters:

- valid zero-rated sales;
- attributable and unutilized input VAT;
- compliant VAT invoices and required supporting documents;
- filing within the statutory two-year period; and
- compliance with the current administrative claim procedure.

Under the current Section 112 framework, VAT refund claims are risk-classified, and the Commissioner generally has **90 days** from submission of the prescribed complete documents to act. A denial or inaction is governed by the current reconsideration and Court of Tax Appeals appeal periods.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Taxpayer's VAT registration, legal qualification of the sales as zero-rated, attribution and utilization of input VAT, invoice compliance, quarter of sale, and filing date.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 112, as amended by RA 11976 and RA 12066; current BIR VAT-refund regulations and documentary rules.

    **PASS requires:** Uses “zero-rated,” states that a refund or tax credit may be claimed for attributable unutilized input VAT, and mentions the two-year filing rule and 90-day processing framework.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Calls export sales VAT-exempt, states that input VAT is unrecoverable, or describes the refund as unconditional.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes

## Q4 — VAT on Doctors' Professional Fees

    **Category:** VAT / Professional Services  
    **Canonical question:** Are doctors' professional fees subject to VAT?  
    **Correct short answer:** They can be. The VAT exemption for medical, dental, hospital, and veterinary services expressly excludes services rendered by professionals.

    ### Validated answer

    Independent professional services rendered by doctors are **not covered by the medical-services VAT exemption** because Section 109(G) expressly excludes services rendered by professionals.

Accordingly:

- A doctor who is VAT-registered or required to register because taxable gross sales exceed the VAT threshold generally charges **12% VAT** on professional fees.
- A non-VAT doctor below the threshold is generally subject to the applicable percentage tax unless another exemption applies.
- The exempt category principally covers qualifying medical, dental, hospital, and veterinary services of hospitals, clinics, and similar institutions—not the independent professional fee merely because the professional is a doctor.

VAT treatment is separate from expanded withholding tax on the professional fee.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Whether the fee belongs to the physician or hospital, the professional's VAT status and sales level, and whether another special exemption applies.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Sections 108 and 109(G); RR 16-2005; RMC 6-2003.

    **PASS requires:** States that professional services are excluded from the exemption and correctly makes VAT depend on VAT-registration status or the threshold.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Categorically states that doctors' professional fees are VAT-exempt.

    **Recency-sensitive:** No

## Q5 — VAT on Imports Used for Export Production

    **Category:** VAT / Importation / CREATE MORE  
    **Canonical question:** What is the VAT rate on importation of goods used to manufacture export products?  
    **Correct short answer:** The general rule is 12% import VAT, but qualifying goods imported by an export-oriented enterprise may be VAT-exempt under CREATE MORE.

    ### Validated answer

    Importations are generally subject to **12% VAT**. However, under CREATE MORE, the importation of goods by an **export-oriented enterprise** may be VAT-exempt when:

- export sales are at least **70% of total annual production** for the relevant statutory period;
- the imported goods are **directly attributable** to the export activity; and
- compliance with the export threshold is determined through the prescribed DTI Export Marketing Bureau process.

This statutory pathway is not limited to PEZA- or BOI-registered enterprises. A separate incentive framework applies to registered business enterprises, but IPA registration is not the only possible basis for the exemption.

TINA must not assume that every imported input eventually used in an exported product is automatically exempt.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Export ratio, prior-year production, direct attribution, DTI determination, importer identity, IPA/RBE status, and the nature of the imported goods.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Sections 107 and 109(1)(dd), as amended by RA 12066.

    **PASS requires:** States the 12% general rule and the CREATE MORE exemption, including the 70% test, direct attribution, and non-IPA possibility.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Says all such imports are automatically 12% VAT, all are automatically exempt, or PEZA/BOI registration is indispensable.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes

## Q6 — Non-VAT Seller Issuing a VAT Invoice

    **Category:** VAT / Invoicing  
    **Canonical question:** Can a non-VAT-registered seller issue a VAT invoice?  
    **Correct short answer:** No. Doing so creates VAT liability and penalties.

    ### Validated answer

    A non-VAT-registered seller should issue a **Non-VAT Invoice**, not a VAT Invoice.

If a non-VAT person issues an invoice showing the person's TIN followed by “VAT,” the issuer is generally liable, in addition to applicable percentage tax, for:

- VAT under Section 106 or 108 **without any input-tax credit**; and
- the **50% surcharge** under Section 248(B).

The purchaser may recognize the VAT as input tax only when the statutory material information requirements are satisfied. The seller's error is therefore not cured merely because the buyer is VAT-registered.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** The invoice wording, seller's BIR registration, material invoice information, and purchaser's VAT status.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 113(D) and Section 248(B), as amended by RA 11976; RR 7-2024; RMC 77-2024.

    **PASS requires:** Answers no and states VAT liability without input credit plus the 50% surcharge.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Says it is permissible or omits the principal consequence.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes

## Q7 — Input VAT Used for VATable and Exempt Sales

    **Category:** VAT / Input Tax  
    **Canonical question:** How is input VAT treated when goods or services are used for both VATable and VAT-exempt sales?  
    **Correct short answer:** Directly attribute input VAT where possible; apportion common input VAT ratably.

    ### Validated answer

    Input VAT must first be **directly attributed**:

- Input VAT directly attributable to VATable or zero-rated sales is generally creditable, subject to the applicable rules.
- Input VAT directly attributable to VAT-exempt sales is not creditable and is generally treated as part of cost or expense, subject to the income-tax rules.
- Common input VAT that cannot be directly attributed must be **allocated ratably** between VATable/zero-rated and exempt sales using the prescribed allocation method.

TINA must not allow all common input VAT or disallow all of it without performing the required attribution and allocation.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Direct attribution, composition of sales, applicable allocation period, and supporting VAT invoices.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 110(B); RR 16-2005.

    **PASS requires:** Explains direct attribution, non-creditability for exempt sales, and ratable apportionment of common input VAT.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Says all input VAT is creditable or none is creditable.

    **Recency-sensitive:** No

## Q8 — VAT on Residential Lease at ₱15,000

    **Category:** VAT / Lease  
    **Canonical question:** Is leasing a residential unit at ₱15,000 per month subject to VAT?  
    **Correct short answer:** No. A residential unit with monthly rent not exceeding ₱15,000 is VAT-exempt.

    ### Validated answer

    The lease of a residential unit with a monthly rental **not exceeding ₱15,000 per unit** is exempt from VAT.

The exemption is tested per residential unit and per monthly rental. It is not lost merely because the lessor's aggregate annual rental income from multiple qualifying units exceeds the general VAT-registration threshold.

The lessor remains subject to applicable income tax and must properly classify any nonqualifying units, commercial spaces, parking, utilities, or separately billed services.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Monthly rent per unit, residential use, separately billed components, and whether other leased properties are involved.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 109(Q); RR 16-2005.

    **PASS requires:** States the ₱15,000 per-unit monthly threshold and that qualifying rent is VAT-exempt regardless of the lessor's aggregate rental income.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Subjects the unit to VAT solely because rent equals ₱15,000 or because aggregate income exceeds ₱3 million.

    **Recency-sensitive:** No

## Q9 — Joint Income Tax Return of Spouses

    **Category:** Income Tax / Filing  
    **Canonical question:** Can spouses who both earn compensation income file a joint income tax return?  
    **Correct short answer:** Yes, if an income tax return is required. However, many purely compensation-income spouses qualify for substituted filing and therefore do not file a separate annual return at all.

    ### Validated answer

    The categorical statement that the Philippines has no joint-filing mechanism is wrong.

For spouses earning purely compensation income:

- First determine whether each spouse qualifies for **substituted filing**. If the conditions are satisfied, no BIR Form 1700 is filed.
- If an annual return is required, BIR Form 1700 accommodates the income and tax information of the taxpayer and spouse and recognizes joint or separate filing.
- Each spouse's income tax is still **computed separately** on their respective taxable income.
- Where joint filing is impracticable, separate returns may be filed under the governing rules.

For spouses with business or professional income, current Section 51(D), as amended by EOPT, generally requires one return including both spouses' income, subject to the impracticability exception and BIR consolidation.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Whether each spouse has one or multiple employers, correctness of withholding, other income, substituted-filing eligibility, and whether joint filing is practicable.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 51(D), as amended by RA 11976; RR 3-2002; RR 11-2018; current BIR Form 1700 and instructions.

    **PASS requires:** Recognizes joint filing when a return is required, separately computed tax, and substituted-filing qualifications.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Says joint filing is never allowed or ignores substituted filing.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes

## Q10 — Corporate Income Tax Rate under CREATE

    **Category:** Income Tax / Corporations  
    **Canonical question:** What is the income tax rate for a domestic corporation under CREATE?  
    **Correct short answer:** Generally 25%, or 20% when both the net-taxable-income and total-asset conditions are met.

    ### Validated answer

    The regular corporate income tax rate for a domestic corporation is generally **25%** of taxable income.

A **20%** rate applies only when both conditions are satisfied for the taxable year:

1. net taxable income does not exceed **₱5 million**; and
2. total assets do not exceed **₱100 million**, excluding the land on which the corporation's office, plant, and equipment are situated.

Registered business enterprises under the enhanced deductions regime are governed by the separate 20% rate on taxable income from registered projects or activities under CREATE MORE.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Net taxable income, total assets, excluded land, taxpayer type, and RBE status.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 27(A), as amended by RA 11534 and RA 12066.

    **PASS requires:** States 25%, the 20% alternative, and both statutory conditions.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Uses the old 30% rate or applies 20% when only one condition is met.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes

## Q11 — Taxability of ₱80,000 13th-Month Pay

    **Category:** Income Tax / Compensation  
    **Canonical question:** Is ₱80,000 of 13th-month pay received by a rank-and-file employee taxable?  
    **Correct short answer:** Generally no, provided the employee's aggregate 13th-month pay and other covered benefits do not exceed ₱90,000.

    ### Validated answer

    Thirteenth-month pay and other benefits covered by Section 32(B)(7)(e) are excluded from gross income up to an **aggregate annual ceiling of ₱90,000**.

If ₱80,000 is the employee's total covered benefit for the year, it is non-taxable. If the employee receives additional covered benefits that cause the aggregate to exceed ₱90,000, only the excess is taxable, subject to the applicable compensation rules.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Aggregate annual 13th-month pay and other covered benefits, not only the single payment.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 32(B)(7)(e), as amended by RA 10963.

    **PASS requires:** States the ₱90,000 aggregate ceiling and treats ₱80,000 as non-taxable on the stated assumption.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Uses the old ₱82,000 threshold or taxes the entire ₱80,000.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes

## Q12 — ITR Requirement at ₱250,000 Compensation

    **Category:** Income Tax / Filing  
    **Canonical question:** Is an individual with ₱250,000 gross compensation income in 2024 required to file an income tax return?  
    **Correct short answer:** Usually no if the person has purely compensation income and satisfies the statutory no-filing or substituted-filing conditions, but the amount alone is not enough to decide.

    ### Validated answer

    A person with purely compensation income not exceeding the applicable ₱250,000 zero-tax band will commonly have no income tax due. However, TINA must not equate the amount alone with substituted filing.

The answer depends on whether:

- the person earned purely compensation income;
- compensation came from only one employer or multiple employers;
- tax was correctly withheld;
- the employee has other income requiring a return; and
- the employee falls within the current substituted-filing rules.

An employee with multiple employers or incorrect withholding may still be required to file BIR Form 1700 even if little or no final tax is due.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Number of employers, withholding accuracy, other income, residency, and substituted-filing qualification.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 51(A)(2); RR 3-2002; RR 11-2018; current BIR substituted-filing rules.

    **PASS requires:** Explains that the result is generally no but conditions it on pure compensation and filing-status facts.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Says all persons at ₱250,000 must file or that substituted filing automatically applies regardless of employer and withholding facts.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes

## Q13 — Eight Percent Income Tax Option

    **Category:** Income Tax / Self-Employed  
    **Canonical question:** Can a self-employed individual with gross sales below ₱3 million opt for the 8% income tax rate?  
    **Correct short answer:** Yes, if the individual is qualified, non-VAT, and timely elects the option.

    ### Validated answer

    A qualified self-employed individual or professional whose gross sales and other non-operating income do not exceed the VAT threshold may elect the **8% income tax rate**.

For a taxpayer earning purely from self-employment or practice of profession, the 8% tax is generally applied to gross sales and other non-operating income **in excess of ₱250,000**, in lieu of graduated income tax and Section 116 percentage tax.

For a **mixed-income earner**, the ₱250,000 reduction is not deducted from business gross sales under the 8% option because the zero-rate band is already reflected in the graduated tax computation on compensation income.

The option must be timely signified for the taxable year and, once elected, is generally irrevocable for that year. VAT-registered taxpayers and other excluded taxpayers cannot use the option.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** VAT status, gross sales plus non-operating income, pure or mixed income, timely election, and excluded taxpayer status.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 24(A)(2)(b); RR 8-2018; RMO 23-2018.

    **PASS requires:** States the 8% rate, VAT-threshold condition, in-lieu-of treatment, election requirement, and the different ₱250,000 treatment for pure versus mixed income.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Applies 8% to corporations, net income, a VAT-registered person, or automatically deducts ₱250,000 for a mixed-income earner.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes

## Q14 — Final Tax on Peso Savings Interest

    **Category:** Income Tax / Passive Income  
    **Canonical question:** Is interest on a Philippine peso savings account subject to final withholding tax?  
    **Correct short answer:** Yes, generally at 20%.

    ### Validated answer

    Interest, yield, or other monetary benefit from a Philippine currency bank deposit is generally subject to **20% final withholding tax**.

CMEPA standardized the current treatment of covered deposit and similar investment income, subject to statutory effectivity and transition rules. For a straightforward peso savings account, the correct current answer is 20% final tax, withheld by the bank.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Deposit type, holder's taxpayer classification, issuance or placement date where transition rules matter, and any statutory exemption.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 24(B)(1), as amended by RA 12214; RR 21-2025 and related CMEPA issuances.

    **PASS requires:** Answers yes and states 20% final withholding tax.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Calls ordinary peso savings interest exempt or subjects it to graduated income tax.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes

## Q15 — Minimum Corporate Income Tax

    **Category:** Income Tax / Corporations  
    **Canonical question:** How is MCIT computed and when does it apply?  
    **Correct short answer:** MCIT is generally 2% of gross income and applies beginning on the fourth taxable year immediately following the year business operations commenced, when it exceeds regular corporate income tax.

    ### Validated answer

    The minimum corporate income tax is generally **2% of gross income** as defined by the Tax Code.

It applies:

- beginning on the **fourth taxable year immediately following** the taxable year in which the corporation commenced business operations; and
- when MCIT is greater than the regular corporate income tax for the taxable year.

The temporary 1% MCIT relief under CREATE has expired. Excess MCIT may be carried forward and credited against regular corporate income tax for the next three taxable years, subject to the statutory rules.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Commencement year, taxable-year count, gross-income base, regular CIT, exempt or incentive status, and prior excess MCIT.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 27(E), as amended by RA 11534.

    **PASS requires:** States 2%, gross income, exact commencement rule, and the higher-than-regular-tax condition.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Uses 1% as the current rate or says MCIT begins simply in the fourth operating year without the statutory formulation.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes

## Q16 — Tax on Prizes

    **Category:** Income Tax / Passive Income  
    **Canonical question:** Are prizes won in a Philippine competition taxable?  
    **Correct short answer:** Generally yes. Prizes exceeding ₱10,000 are generally subject to 20% final tax; prizes of ₱10,000 or less generally enter regular taxable income, subject to statutory exclusions.

    ### Validated answer

    For an individual, a prize exceeding **₱10,000** is generally subject to **20% final tax**. A prize of ₱10,000 or less is generally included in gross income and taxed under the regular rules.

However, TINA must recognize statutory exclusions, including qualifying awards made primarily in recognition of religious, charitable, scientific, educational, artistic, literary, or civic achievement when the statutory conditions are met, and qualifying sports prizes sanctioned under the Tax Code.

PCSO and Lotto winnings have their own statutory treatment and must not be described using the old blanket-exemption rule.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Amount, nature of competition or award, selection conditions, required future services, sports-sanction status, and whether the prize is a PCSO/Lotto winning.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Sections 24(B)(1) and 32(B)(7)(c)-(d), as amended by RA 10963 and RA 12214.

    **PASS requires:** States the ₱10,000 division, 20% final tax above it, regular treatment at or below it, and acknowledges statutory exclusions.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Says all prizes are exempt or all are automatically subject to one treatment without checking exclusions.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes

## Q17 — Dividend Paid to an NRA-NETB

    **Category:** Income Tax / Nonresident Alien  
    **Canonical question:** What tax applies to a dividend paid to a nonresident alien not engaged in trade or business in the Philippines?  
    **Correct short answer:** Generally 25% final withholding tax on Philippine-source income, subject to a lower applicable treaty rate.

    ### Validated answer

    A nonresident alien individual not engaged in trade or business in the Philippines is generally subject to **25% final tax** on gross Philippine-source income, including dividends from a domestic corporation.

An applicable tax treaty may reduce the rate if the beneficial owner and treaty-relief requirements are satisfied. TINA should not apply a treaty rate without identifying the country, beneficial ownership, and required documentation.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Residence country, source and payer of the dividend, beneficial ownership, and treaty documentation.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 25(B), as amended; applicable Philippine tax treaty and treaty-relief rules.

    **PASS requires:** States 25%, Philippine-source income, and treaty qualification.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Uses the resident-individual dividend rate or another rate without a valid treaty basis.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes

## Q18 — Optional Standard Deduction

    **Category:** Income Tax / Deductions  
    **Canonical question:** What is the OSD rate for individuals and corporations?  
    **Correct short answer:** 40% for both, but the tax base differs.

    ### Validated answer

    The optional standard deduction is **40%**, but the base is different:

- For an individual, OSD is 40% of **gross sales or gross receipts**.
- For a corporation, OSD is 40% of **gross income**.

The election and filing requirements must be met, and OSD replaces itemized deductions for the relevant taxable year.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Taxpayer type, accounting and income classification, and timely OSD election.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 34(L).

    **PASS requires:** States 40% and correctly distinguishes the individual and corporate bases.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Uses the same base for both taxpayer types.

    **Recency-sensitive:** No

## Q19 — Capital Gains Tax on Unlisted Shares

    **Category:** Capital Gains / Shares  
    **Canonical question:** What is the tax rate on the sale of shares not traded on a stock exchange?  
    **Correct short answer:** Generally 15% final tax on net capital gain.

    ### Validated answer

    The sale, exchange, or disposition of shares not traded on a local or foreign stock exchange is generally subject to **15% final tax on net capital gain**, under the taxpayer-specific provisions of the current Tax Code.

After CMEPA, the rule extends, where the statute applies, to shares of domestic or foreign corporations not traded through a local or foreign exchange. Taxpayer residency, corporate status, source rules, and treaty provisions must still be considered.

The old 5%/10% tiered capital-gains rates are obsolete.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Seller type and residency, domestic or foreign shares, exchange status, acquisition cost, selling price, and treaty or special-law treatment.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** Current NIRC Section 24(B)(3) for individuals, Section 27(D)(4) for domestic corporations, and Section 28 as applicable, as amended by RA 12214.

    **PASS requires:** States 15% on net capital gain and does not use the old tiered rates.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Uses 5%/10%, applies the tax to gross selling price, or ignores a material taxpayer/source distinction.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes

## Q20 — Principal-Residence CGT Exemption

    **Category:** Capital Gains / Real Property  
    **Canonical question:** Is the sale of a principal residence subject to capital gains tax, and when may it be exempt?  
    **Correct short answer:** It is generally subject to 6% capital gains tax, but a qualifying individual may obtain exemption by fully using the proceeds for a new principal residence within 18 months and satisfying the other statutory conditions.

    ### Validated answer

    A sale of an individual's principal residence that is a capital asset is generally subject to **6% capital gains tax**. The exemption may apply when:

1. the proceeds are fully used to acquire or construct a new principal residence within **18 calendar months** from the sale;
2. the taxpayer notifies the BIR within **30 days** from the sale through the prescribed process;
3. the exemption is used only **once every ten years**; and
4. the documentary, escrow, and reporting requirements are satisfied.

If only part of the proceeds is used, the unutilized portion is subject to proportionate capital gains tax.

    ### Validated answer

    Oplan Kandado implements the Commissioner's power to suspend business operations and temporarily close an establishment for statutory violations.

Grounds include, depending on the taxpayer:

- failure to issue required invoices;
- failure to file a required VAT return;
- understatement of taxable sales by 30% or more;
- failure of a person required to register for VAT to register; and
- other violations covered by Section 115 and the implementing order.

Closure is temporary and generally lasts for at least **five days**, continuing until the prescribed compliance conditions are satisfied. Due process and the operational procedures in RMO 3-2009 must be observed.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Taxpayer's VAT status, precise violation, measurement period, notices, hearing/compliance procedure, and cure.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 115; RMO 3-2009.

    **PASS requires:** Identifies temporary closure, valid statutory grounds, and minimum closure framework.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Calls it permanent closure or invents grounds outside the governing law.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Moderate

## Q44 — PayPal Payment from a Foreign Client

    **Category:** Income Tax / Source and Residency  
    **Canonical question:** Is a freelancer's PayPal payment from a foreign client taxable in the Philippines?  
    **Correct short answer:** It is taxable to a Philippine resident citizen as part of worldwide income. The payment channel and client's location do not determine the income source.

    ### Validated answer

    A **resident citizen** is taxable on income from sources within and outside the Philippines. Therefore, payment for freelance services is generally taxable even when the client is abroad and payment arrives through PayPal.

For service income, source is generally determined by **where the services are performed**, not where the client resides or where the payment platform is located. If the freelancer performed the work in the Philippines, the income is ordinarily Philippine-source.

For a nonresident citizen, resident alien, or nonresident alien, the taxable-source rules differ. TINA should identify citizenship, tax residence, and place of performance before giving a final classification.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Citizenship, residence, place services were performed, business registration, foreign tax paid, and treaty or foreign-tax-credit facts.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Sections 23 and 42.

    **PASS requires:** States the resident-citizen worldwide-income rule and the place-of-performance source rule.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Says the income is exempt merely because the client or PayPal account is abroad.

    **Recency-sensitive:** No

## Q45 — Corporate Income Tax of a PEZA-Registered Enterprise

    **Category:** Tax Incentives / CREATE MORE  
    **Canonical question:** Are PEZA-registered enterprises subject to corporate income tax?  
    **Correct short answer:** They are not simply tax-exempt. Tax depends on the registered project, enterprise type, elected incentive package, and transition status.

    ### Validated answer

    A PEZA-registered business enterprise is taxed under the current CREATE/CREATE MORE incentive framework and its certificate of registration.

For a **registered export enterprise**, the available packages may include:

- income tax holiday followed by 5% special corporate income tax or enhanced deductions;
- immediate 5% SCIT; or
- immediate enhanced deductions,

subject to statutory conditions and an irrevocable election for the incentive period.

For a **registered domestic market enterprise**, the general options are:

- income tax holiday followed by enhanced deductions; or
- immediate enhanced deductions.

The 5% SCIT is generally an export-enterprise incentive, subject to legacy and special transition rules. Under the enhanced deductions regime, the income-tax rate is generally **20% of taxable income from the registered project or activity**. Income from nonregistered activities is generally subject to the regular tax rules.

Pre-CREATE registrations may remain governed by transition provisions and their certificates.

If TINA discusses VAT on local sales, it must apply **RR 9-2025 as amended by RR 1-2026**. It must not state the original buyer-remit rule without recognizing the exclusions and seller-remit treatment introduced or clarified by RR 1-2026.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Export or domestic-market classification, registration date, certificate terms, registered versus nonregistered income, elected package, incentive period, export ratio, and local-sale type.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Sections 27, 28, 294, 295, and 311, as amended by RA 11534 and RA 12066; RR 9-2025; RR 1-2026; PEZA/FIRB registration documents.

    **PASS requires:** States that tax depends on the incentive regime, distinguishes export enterprises from domestic-market enterprises, mentions ITH/SCIT/EDR, and states the 20% EDR rate.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Says PEZA enterprises are entirely exempt, gives every DME a 5% SCIT option, or discusses RR 9-2025 local-sale VAT without the RR 1-2026 amendment.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Highly sensitive

## Q46 — Sale of Gold to the BSP

    **Category:** VAT / Special Law  
    **Canonical question:** Is the sale of gold by a small-scale miner to the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas subject to VAT?  
    **Correct short answer:** A qualifying sale is VAT-exempt when made by a registered small-scale miner or accredited trader under RA 11256.

    ### Validated answer

    The sale of gold to the BSP is VAT-exempt when the seller and transaction satisfy **RA 11256**, including the registration or accreditation requirements for small-scale miners and traders.

The exemption is not a blanket exemption for every person selling gold to the BSP. TINA must verify the seller's registration/accreditation and compliance with the special-law requirements. The transaction is **VAT-exempt**, not zero-rated.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Seller registration or accreditation, BSP purchase, gold source, and compliance documents.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** RA 11256; applicable implementing rules and BIR guidance, including RMC 40-2020; NIRC Section 109 as applicable.

    **PASS requires:** States VAT exemption and the registered-miner/accredited-trader qualification.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Calls it zero-rated, automatically VATable, or exempt without seller qualification.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Moderate

## Q47 — Donor's Tax Rate and Annual Exemption

    **Category:** Donor's Tax  
    **Canonical question:** What is the donor's tax rate under TRAIN and the exemption threshold?  
    **Correct short answer:** 6% on total net gifts exceeding the aggregate ₱250,000 annual exemption.

    ### Validated answer

    Donor's tax is a flat **6%** on total net gifts made during the calendar year in excess of the aggregate **₱250,000 annual exemption**.

TRAIN removed the former separate rate for gifts to strangers. The taxpayer must aggregate taxable gifts during the calendar year and consider valuation, exempt gifts, marital-property ownership, and prior gifts.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Donor identity and residency, calendar-year prior gifts, donee, valuation, exemptions, and property ownership.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 99, as amended by RA 10963.

    **PASS requires:** States 6%, the aggregate ₱250,000 annual exemption, and removal of the stranger distinction.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Uses the old graduated rates or 30% stranger rate.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes

## Q48 — Bad-Debt Deduction of a Corporation

    **Category:** Income Tax / Deductions  
    **Canonical question:** What conditions must a corporation satisfy to claim a bad debt as a deductible expense?  
    **Correct short answer:** The debt must be valid, business-connected, actually worthless and uncollectible during the taxable year, and charged off, subject to related-party and substantiation rules.

    ### Validated answer

    A bad-debt deduction generally requires:

1. an existing, valid, and legally demandable debt;
2. a debt connected with the taxpayer's trade or business;
3. actual ascertainment of worthlessness and uncollectibility during the taxable year;
4. charge-off from the books during that taxable year;
5. absence of a disqualifying related-party transaction or another statutory bar; and
6. evidence of reasonable collection efforts and the debtor's inability or refusal to pay, unless the facts establish that further collection would be futile.

For an accrual-basis receivable arising from a sale or service, the amount must ordinarily have been previously recognized as income before its later write-off. However, “previously included in gross income” is **not a universal requirement for every bona fide business loan or debt**.

A mere accounting provision or allowance for doubtful accounts is generally not enough.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Origin and validity of debt, accounting method, prior income recognition where applicable, related parties, collection history, debtor solvency, write-off date, and supporting documents.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 34(E); NIRC Section 36(B) as applicable; RR 5-1999; relevant Supreme Court jurisprudence.

    **PASS requires:** States valid debt, business connection, actual worthlessness, charge-off, evidence, and nuanced prior-income rule.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Allows a general reserve, treats prior income inclusion as universally indispensable, or ignores related-party and worthlessness requirements.

    **Recency-sensitive:** No

## Q49 — VAT on Personal Effects of a Returning Resident

    **Category:** VAT / Customs  
    **Canonical question:** Is the importation of personal effects or household goods by a returning resident subject to VAT?  
    **Correct short answer:** It is VAT-exempt only to the extent the goods qualify for the corresponding customs duty-and-tax exemption.

    ### Validated answer

    The VAT exemption is tied to the conditional customs exemption.

Personal and household effects of a qualifying returning resident may be exempt from duties and VAT when the requirements under Section 800(f) of the CMTA and the implementing customs rules are met, including conditions on:

- returning-resident or OFW status;
- noncommercial quantity and personal/household use;
- prohibition against barter, sale, or hire;
- arrival period;
- excluded luxury items, vehicles, aircraft, watercraft, animals, and other nonqualifying goods; and
- applicable value limits and prior availment.

Any excess or nonqualifying goods are subject to the applicable duties and internal revenue taxes.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** Returning-resident/OFW status, length of stay abroad, arrival date, goods, quantity, value, prior availment, and customs declaration.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Section 109(F); RA 10863, Section 800(f); current BOC CAO/CMO and returning-resident guidance.

    **PASS requires:** Links VAT exemption to customs qualification and identifies the principal conditions.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Says all returning-resident effects are unconditionally exempt or all are automatically taxable.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Moderate

## Q50 — Official Receipt versus Invoice after EOPT

    **Category:** Invoicing / EOPT  
    **Canonical question:** What is the current rule on Official Receipts versus Sales Invoices?  
    **Correct short answer:** The former goods-versus-services distinction is obsolete. An invoice is now the principal sales document for both goods and services; an Official Receipt is generally a supplementary payment document.

    ### Validated answer

    Effective under EOPT and its implementing rules:

- A VAT-registered seller issues a **VAT Invoice** for sales of goods, properties, services, and leases.
- A non-VAT seller issues a **Non-VAT Invoice**.
- The document may use a descriptive business label such as **Sales Invoice, Service Invoice, Cash Invoice, Charge Invoice, or Billing Invoice**, provided it is a compliant invoice and contains the required information.
- An **Official Receipt, Payment Receipt, Collection Receipt, or similar document** is generally supplementary evidence of collection, not the principal evidence of the sale.
- Remaining unused Official Receipts could be converted into invoices only under the prescribed transition and stamping rules. An unconverted Official Receipt issued as the sole sales document after the applicable transition is treated as failure to issue the required invoice.
- Input VAT must be supported by a compliant VAT Invoice, subject to the material-information rules.

TINA must not say that only a document literally titled “Sales Invoice” is valid. The controlling legal concept is a compliant **Invoice**.

    **Material facts or qualifications:** VAT status, invoice type and content, date issued, transition conversion, ATP/system registration, and whether the document evidences sale or payment.

    **Controlling and supporting authorities:** NIRC Sections 113 and 237, as amended by RA 11976; RR 7-2024; RR 11-2024; RMC 77-2024.

    **PASS requires:** States that invoices are principal documents for both goods and services, ORs are supplementary, and VAT/Non-VAT invoices and valid descriptive labels are recognized.

    **FAIL / unsafe indicator:** Repeats “OR for services, SI for goods,” or insists that only the literal title “Sales Invoice” is legally valid.

    **Recency-sensitive:** Yes
