# Expert-Level Philippine Tax Question

## Question

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) issued a Final Assessment Notice (FAN) against a corporation. However:

- The audit was conducted by revenue officers who were not named in a valid original, amended, or supplemental Letter of Authority (LOA).
- The FAN did not clearly explain the specific factual and legal bases of the assessment.
- The corporation failed to protest the FAN within 30 days from receipt.

The BIR argues that, regardless of these defects, the assessment became final, executory, and demandable because the corporation did not file a timely protest.

**Is the BIR correct?**

## Updated and Validated Answer

**The BIR is not necessarily correct. The decisive issue is whether there was a valid assessment capable of becoming final. However, the taxpayer's failure to protest creates a serious procedural and jurisdictional risk.**

Section 228 of the National Internal Revenue Code generally requires a taxpayer to protest a Final Assessment Notice within **30 days from receipt**. Failure to protest a valid assessment within that period ordinarily makes it final, executory, and demandable.

Nevertheless, procedural finality does not ordinarily cure an assessment that was void from the beginning. A distinction must therefore be made between an assessment that is merely erroneous and one that is legally void.

## 1. Audit without a valid LOA

A Letter of Authority is the legal authority granted to designated revenue officers to examine a taxpayer's books and accounting records. Revenue officers who are not named in a valid original, amended, or supplemental LOA generally have no authority to conduct the examination.

The unauthorized substitution or replacement of revenue officers without the issuance of the required LOA violates the taxpayer's right to due process and usurps the statutory authority of the Commissioner of Internal Revenue or the Commissioner's duly authorized representative. An assessment resulting from such an unauthorized examination is generally void.

**Authority:** [Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. McDonald's Philippines Realty Corporation, G.R. No. 242670, May 10, 2021](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2021/may2021/gr_242670_2021.html)

The Supreme Court has likewise explained that the absence of authority to conduct the audit affects the validity of the assessment itself. A void assessment "bears no fruit."

**Related authority:** [Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. Autohub, Inc., G.R. No. 241848, May 10, 2021](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2021/may2021/gr_241848_2021.html)

## 2. Failure to state the factual and legal bases

Section 228 requires the taxpayer to be informed in writing of the law and facts on which an assessment is made. This requirement is substantive, not merely formal.

Providing only figures, computations, conclusions, or general references to provisions of law may be insufficient when the documents do not adequately explain why the taxpayer is liable. A FAN that fails to comply with this requirement is void for violation of the taxpayer's right to due process.

**Authority:** [Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. Liquigaz Philippines Corporation, G.R. No. 215957, November 28, 2016](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2016/nov2016/gr_215957_2016.html)

## 3. Effect of the failure to protest within 30 days

The 30-day protest requirement clearly applies when the taxpayer receives a **valid assessment**. If the assessment is valid and the taxpayer merely disputes the BIR's factual findings, interpretation, or computation, failure to file a timely protest ordinarily makes the assessment final, executory, and demandable.

By contrast, an assessment issued without statutory authority or in violation of a substantive due-process requirement is generally treated as void rather than merely erroneous. Such an assessment does not become legally valid simply because the taxpayer remained silent.

However, this principle does **not** mean that a taxpayer may safely ignore a FAN. There is an important distinction between:

- The substantive question of whether the assessment is void; and
- The procedural question of whether the taxpayer has properly invoked the jurisdiction of the Court of Tax Appeals (CTA).

The CTA's jurisdiction generally extends to a decision of the Commissioner on a disputed assessment or another action made appealable by law—not simply to the assessment standing alone. Consequently, a petition filed without a timely administrative protest may be dismissed for lack of jurisdiction.

**Authority:** [Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. Citysuper, Inc., G.R. No. 239464, May 17, 2021](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2021/may2021/gr_239464_2021.html)

## 4. Can the taxpayer wait for BIR collection action?

It is too broad to say that a taxpayer may always ignore a void FAN, wait for the BIR to begin collection, and then proceed directly to the CTA.

Under particular circumstances, a Warrant of Distraint and Levy or another collection action may constitute a final or constructive denial that can be appealed to the CTA. In one case, the Supreme Court treated a warrant as the constructive and final denial of the taxpayer's belated protest, from which the applicable appeal period was reckoned.

**Authority:** [Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. South Entertainment Gallery, Inc., G.R. No. 225809, March 17, 2021](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2021/mar2021/gr_225809_2021.html)

That ruling does not establish a universal right to disregard a FAN and wait for collection. Whether a collection action is independently appealable, and when the period to appeal begins, depends on the documents issued, the taxpayer's prior actions, and the specific procedural history.

## Correct Conclusion

The BIR's position is correct only if the FAN was legally valid. A valid but erroneous assessment may become final, executory, and demandable through the taxpayer's failure to protest. A genuinely void assessment does not become valid merely through the taxpayer's silence.

Nevertheless, it is legally unsafe to state categorically that the 30-day period never commenced or that the corporation may automatically wait for collection and then challenge the assessment before the CTA. The CTA must have jurisdiction over an appealable decision or action, and the taxpayer must comply with the period applicable to that remedy.

> **Practical rule:** A taxpayer should always file a protest within 30 days of receiving a FAN—even if the assessment appears void—and specifically raise the LOA, due-process, prescription, and substantive defects. This preserves both the substantive defenses and access to the proper administrative and judicial remedies.

## Short Model Answer

**The BIR is not necessarily correct.** An assessment resulting from an audit conducted by revenue officers without a valid LOA is generally void. A FAN that fails to inform the taxpayer in writing of its specific factual and legal bases is likewise void under Section 228 of the Tax Code. A void assessment does not ordinarily become valid merely because the taxpayer failed to protest it within 30 days.

However, the corporation cannot safely assume that it may ignore the FAN and proceed directly to the CTA when the BIR attempts collection. The CTA's jurisdiction generally requires an appealable decision or action of the Commissioner, and failure to make a timely administrative protest may create a jurisdictional bar. Although certain collection actions may constitute an appealable final or constructive denial under particular circumstances, that result depends on the specific procedural facts.

Therefore, the proper and safest course is to protest the FAN within 30 days, expressly raise the LOA and due-process defects, and preserve the right to appeal.
